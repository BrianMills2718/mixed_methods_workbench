Variant guard: exactly three anonymous questionnaire rounds are frozen. Round feedback includes aggregate distributions and anonymized rationales; panelists retain their own judgments. Consensus is a described panel state, not truth, representativeness, or a policy decision; stable dissensus is a valid output.

Policy-Delphi variant locator (added 2026-10-08 per control review): Murray Turoff, “The design of a policy Delphi,” *Technological Forecasting* 2(2), 1970, pp. 149–171, DOI [10.1016/0040-1625(70)90161-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-1625(70)90161-7) (metadata confirmed through Crossref on 2026-10-08). The same author’s chapter “The Policy Delphi” appears in Linstone and Turoff (eds.), *The Delphi Method*; its chapter number and pages were not re-confirmed. This source anchors the move rules that differ from a consensus Delphi: the monitor team formulates the issue and initial options (`design`); the panel spans informed advocates and referees with opposing positions (`panel`); round one adds options and pro/con arguments and rates them on desirability, feasibility, importance and confidence scales (`round1`); feedback preserves opposing arguments (`feedback1`); round two elicits reasons for disagreement (`round2`); round three re-evaluates options (`round3`); and interpretation retains opposing positions (`interpret`). Because the primary text could not be read this session, these features rest on the article’s bibliographic record and on secondary accounts of Turoff’s design (for example the six policy-Delphi phases and the four rating scales), and anchors are article-level; see `p12.u07`. RAND TL-A3082-1 remains the conduct authority for round mechanics, stability, missingness and reporting.
