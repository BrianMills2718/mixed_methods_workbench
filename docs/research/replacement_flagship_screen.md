# Replacement Policy Flagship Screen

**Screened:** 2026-08-13

## Decision

Advance **New York City's Congestion Relief Zone tolling program** to an exact
question-and-corpus freeze for the first cohesive Evidence-to-Action MVP.

This is a candidate-selection decision, not corpus acceptance or authorization
to run Qualitative Coding, a quantitative model, Process Tracing, or a product
implementation. Those begin only after the exact public sources, data snapshot,
time boundary, analytical question, and permitted claims are frozen.

Retain London's 2023 ULEZ expansion as the runner-up. Keep Mist Trail as a
secondary pre-decision option-appraisal example.

The first-year report is particularly useful because it documents both results
and limitations. It constructs seasonal monthly traffic baselines, discloses
that historical records cannot always separate tolled-zone entries from
excluded-roadway traffic, uses estimated/proprietary vehicle-miles-traveled
data, treats transit recovery as multi-causal, and declines definitive air-
quality conclusions where coverage is insufficient.

## Proposed bounded MVP question

> After the first year of New York City's congestion-pricing program, what
> effects are supported by the public record, which predicted benefits or
> distributional risks remain unresolved, and what should decision-makers
> retain, change, or monitor next?

This is deliberately multi-part. It permits description, explanation,
quantitative evaluation, and decision appraisal without pretending those are
one inferential operation or a fixed ladder.

## Coherent analytical path

1. **Frame the decision.** Extract the statutory objectives, alternatives,
   chosen toll design, expected effects, mitigations, affected groups, and
   decision chronology from the final EA/FONSI and toll-design record.
2. **Describe stakeholder concerns.** Use QC to analyze the six public-hearing
   transcripts with exact speaker/passage anchors, including support,
   opposition, proposed alternatives, distributional concerns, and
   contradictions. The result describes the hearing record, not public opinion
   prevalence.
3. **Extract testable expectations.** Convert selected pre-implementation
   predictions into explicit measures and time/scope conditions without
   treating the extraction as empirical evidence.
4. **Measure observed change.** Use a quantitative engine on frozen public
   traffic and ridership data. The design must handle seasonality, the January
   2025 intervention boundary, other contemporaneous changes, missing or
   proprietary measures, and the distinction between association and causal
   attribution.
5. **Appraise predicted versus observed outcomes.** Compare the final EA's
   expected effects, stakeholder concerns, and observed first-year evidence.
   Preserve conflicts between agency conclusions and independently computed
   results.
6. **Recommend or refuse.** Produce a criteria- and values-explicit appraisal
   of what to retain, change, or monitor. Refuse conclusions that require
   unavailable distributional, air-quality, cost, or causal evidence.
7. **Preserve the investigation.** Keep exact sources, transformations,
   judgments, method results, unresolved questions, and later updates as one
   navigable investigation.

Process Tracing is optional, not required to make the vertical real. Use it
only if an independently reviewed source design identifies a discriminating
question about how a particular toll feature, pause, mitigation, or decision
occurred. Do not relabel the general policy chronology as Process Tracing.

## Why this is better than the alternatives screened

### London ULEZ expansion — runner-up

Transport for London publishes a strong 2022 report-to-Mayor package: the
consultation survey, code frame, consultation analysis, stakeholder summaries,
integrated impact assessments, and a representative poll. The 2023 expansion
was implemented and has monitoring reports. It remains an excellent fallback.

New York ranks first because its official archive more directly exposes
verbatim public-hearing transcripts, its state open-data platform exposes
program-specific and daily machine-readable measures, and a first-year
evaluation now joins the design and implementation records.

### Cuban Missile Crisis / FRUS — Process-Tracing-heavy fallback

The official *Foreign Relations of the United States* 1961–1963, Volume XI
corpus is unusually strong for qualitative analysis and Process Tracing. A
fresh check found 362 historical documents and 28 editorial notes with stable
document identifiers, decision alternatives, dissent, the quarantine decision,
negotiations, implementation, missile and bomber removal, and aftermath. It is
the better candidate if the immediate product objective becomes deep
within-case causal explanation.

It does not outrank New York for the cohesive MVP. Its proposed quantitative
strand—measuring validated option-framing and escalation-risk categories by
document, day, actor, or source group—would still be derived from the same
diplomatic text corpus, and it has not yet shown that the computation would
change the integrated conclusion. New York already supplies a distinct
operational-data strand and observed policy outcomes. Preserve FRUS as a
fallback rather than running another delta gate before the NYC source freeze.

### Mist Trail — retained secondary case

Mist Trail has a real EA, operations appendix, civic-engagement summary, and
adaptive-management design. It still lacks the completed decision, raw
stakeholder text, and quantitative outcome strand needed for the sole MVP
flagship.

### Existing Open Science/P5 journey — integration test only

P5 remains useful proof that the Workbench can join method-owned qualitative
and Process Tracing artifacts. Its anonymized interviews and specialized
research question are not a strong general policy-analysis flagship.

## Remaining source-freeze blockers

The New York case is not ready for execution until the next gate resolves:

1. **Exact question and time boundary.** The program changed between the 2023
   environmental decision, the 2024 toll decision/pause, and the January 2025
   launch. Freeze which decision and which first-year period are in scope.
2. **Corpus size.** Start with the six 2022 hearing transcripts and the minimum
   decision documents; do not ingest the entire archive by default.
3. **Data snapshot.** Export immutable versions of the two open datasets,
   record query parameters and digests, and identify which measures predate
   the intervention.
4. **Rights and attribution.** The open-data pages do not state a dataset
   license in the observed metadata. Confirm permitted reuse and required
   attribution before bundling data or publishing derived artifacts.
5. **Replicability.** Exclude or clearly separate analyses that depend on
   proprietary StreetLight data unless a public substitute supports the same
   question.
6. **Identification design.** Decide which claims are descriptive trends and
   which, if any, warrant interrupted-time-series, comparison-series, or other
   causal language. Do not inherit the agency report's attribution by default.
7. **Current legal/policy status.** Freeze the status and chronology from
   primary sources; ongoing litigation or policy changes must not silently
   alter the case after corpus freeze.
