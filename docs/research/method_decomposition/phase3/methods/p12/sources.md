Variant guard: exactly three anonymous questionnaire rounds are frozen. Round feedback includes aggregate distributions and anonymized rationales; panelists retain their own judgments. Consensus is a described panel state, not truth, representativeness, or a policy decision; stable dissensus is a valid output.

Page anchors for the move rules that differ from a consensus Delphi:
- `design`: the monitor team pre-formulates the obvious issues and seeds an initial range of options that respondents may add to, which is what lets the six communication phases fit a three-round format (p. 84).
- `panel`: a policy issue has “no experts, only informed advocates and referees” (p. 80); participants must be informed people representative of the many sides of the issue (p. 84).
- `round1`: positions on each item and underlying assumptions are asked in the first round (p. 84); rating scales for importance, desirability, confidence and feasibility must be defined (p. 85), with their levels on pp. 86–87.
- `feedback1`: written comments and additions are abstracted carefully and duplicates removed; second-round material is typically five to ten times the first round’s (p. 88).
- `round2`: phase 4, exploring and obtaining the reasons for disagreements (p. 84).
- `round3`: phase 6, re-evaluating the options (p. 84); material is reordered by average vote (options by desirability, arguments by importance), the summary flags polarized, flat and skewed distributions, and in a three-round exercise the revote is taken on the third round (p. 88).
- `interpret`: the Policy Delphi seeks the strongest possible opposing views; it is a tool for analysing policy issues, not a mechanism for making a decision, and consensus is not the prime objective (p. 80).

Turoff states that the six phases would in principle need five rounds and that new material raised by respondents in a three-round format “will not get the same complete treatment” as the monitor team’s initial topics (p. 84); see `p12.u07`. RAND TL-A3082-1 remains the conduct authority for round mechanics, stability, missingness and reporting.
